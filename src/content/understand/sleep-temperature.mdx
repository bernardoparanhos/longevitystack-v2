## Mechanism

Sleep onset is not triggered by darkness or by fatigue alone. It is gated by a measurable drop in core body temperature, and that drop is a heat-transfer problem before it is a neurological one.

The body dumps heat through **arteriovenous anastomoses** — direct shunts between arteries and veins concentrated in the palms, soles and face. When they open, warm blood bypasses the capillary bed and flows straight to the surface, where it radiates. Core temperature falls. Sleep pressure converts into sleep.

This produces the finding that confuses most people: **warm feet make you fall asleep faster in a cold room**. Warming the extremities opens the shunts. Cooling the room gives the released heat somewhere to go.

## The thermal bands

Ambient temperature is not a preference. Each band produces a different, measurable outcome in sleep architecture.

<ThermalScale />

| Ambient band | Thermoregulatory state | Effect on sleep architecture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| < 16 °C · < 61 °F | Vasoconstriction | Heat loss blocked. Onset delayed, more awakenings. |
| 16–18 °C · 61–64 °F | Cool, compensable | Works with warm extremities. Cold feet cancel the benefit. |
| **18–19 °C · 64–66 °F** | **Optimal gradient** | **Fastest onset, longest slow-wave duration.** |
| 19–22 °C · 66–72 °F | Neutral | Tolerable. Core drop is slower; deep sleep shortens. |
| 22–24 °C · 72–75 °F | Warm load | REM fragments first. Measurable before it is felt. |
| > 24 °C · > 75 °F | Thermal stress | Sweating, arousals, both deep and REM suppressed. |

Measured at mattress height, not at the thermostat — the difference is routinely 1–3 °C.     <li>Whether 18 °C is optimal for everyone or an average that hides real variance by body composition, age and menopausal status. Almost certainly the latter.</li>
    <li>Whether dynamic surface cooling outperforms a well-set static room, or only compensates for a badly set one. No independent trial has compared them directly.</li>
    <li>Long-term effects. Every trial cited here is nights-to-weeks. Nobody has run a thermal sleep protocol for a year and measured anything.</li>

## FAQ

**Is 18 °C too cold if I sleep alone under a thin duvet?**
Then the bedding is the variable, not the room. Insulation and ambient temperature trade against each other; hold the room and adjust the tog.

**Does a fan help, or is it just noise?**
Both, and both help. Air movement raises evaporative loss at the skin, which is a real thermal effect independent of the masking noise.

**My partner needs it warmer. What actually works?**
Separate duvets first — it is free and resolves most cases. Dual-zone surface cooling (see [Eight Sleep Pod 4](/gear/eight-sleep-pod-4/)) only if that fails.

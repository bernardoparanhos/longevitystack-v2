## The protocol

<ol style="margin: 0 0 36px; padding-left: 0; list-style: none; display: flex; flex-direction: column; gap: 18px;">
  <li class="ls-protocol-step"><span class="t">T−120</span><span style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.55;">Drop the thermostat. Rooms lose heat slowly; set it two hours out so it is at 18 °C when you lie down, not two hours later.</span></li>
  <li class="ls-protocol-step"><span class="t">T−90</span><span style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.55;">Hot bath or shower, 40–42 °C, at least 10 min. The rebound vasodilation — not the heat itself — is the mechanism.</span></li>
  <li class="ls-protocol-step"><span class="t">T−30</span><span style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.55;">Socks on if your feet run cold. This is the single cheapest intervention on the page and the one most people refuse to try.</span></li>
  <li class="ls-protocol-step"><span class="t">T−0</span><span style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.55;">Breathable bedding, hands and face uncovered. Duvet tog matters more than the thermostat if the duvet traps the heat you just released.</span></li>
  <li class="ls-protocol-step"><span class="t">T+240</span><span style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.55;">Allow a small rise toward morning. A flat setpoint all night is not what the circadian curve asks for.</span></li>
</ol>

If your room cannot hold 18 °C on its own — a partner, a climate, a shared thermostat — see the [Eight Sleep Pod 4](/gear/eight-sleep-pod-4/) review, or start with a [logging thermometer](/gear/logging-thermometer/) to confirm your real number first.

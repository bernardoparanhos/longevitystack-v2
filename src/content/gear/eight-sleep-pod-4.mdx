## Who should buy it

- Two people who need different surface temperatures on the same mattress. This is the case the product genuinely solves and nothing cheaper does.
- Anyone who cannot control ambient temperature — shared thermostat, hot climate, a building with no air conditioning.
- People who already measured their room, confirmed it sits above 22 °C at mattress height, and failed to fix it by other means.

## Who should not

- Anyone who has not yet measured their bedroom. Buy the [$24 logging thermometer](/gear/logging-thermometer/), log two weeks, then decide. Most people who do this never come back to this page.
- Sleepers who already hold 18–19 °C (see [Sleep Thermoregulation](/understand/sleep-thermoregulation/)). The device cannot improve on a room that is already correct.
- Anyone unwilling to pay a recurring subscription for full functionality on hardware they already own outright.

## How it was tested

One unit, 118 consecutive nights, in a bedroom whose ambient temperature was logged independently at mattress height every ten minutes. Sleep staging came from a separate wearable, not from the device's own sensors — a device should not be allowed to grade its own homework.

Baseline: 30 nights without it. Comparison period: 88 nights with it, of which 21 were on a fixed setpoint and the rest on the automatic curve.

## Verdict

**A−.** Works. Priced for a narrow case: two sleepers with conflicting setpoints, or a room that genuinely cannot be brought to 18 °C by other means. Surface range 13–43 °C, 2 independent zones, 41 dB at max, $2,649 plus $199/yr subscription.
